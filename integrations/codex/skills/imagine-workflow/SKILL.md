# Start the Imagine studio for this product, then develop it after handover

Start or update the active project's Imagine studio, following the `imagine_studio` skill.

Call `session.start` with the project first. If your client does not keep the MCP session (a headless run, or a 2026-07-28 client), pass the `session_handle` it returns as the `metaforge_session` argument on every later call, so each call stays in the project and its approval policy applies, including to tools with no `project_id` argument.

Then call `twin.imagine_get_studio`. With no studio, or a draft, build the formgraph config from what the person said, using the skill's skeleton and rules, asking at most three questions. Write it with `twin.imagine_put_studio`. A refusal lists every failed check: fix all of them and write once more. If that is refused too, stop and say which checks still fail.

Then call `twin.imagine_handover_status` and report each item that is missing or assumed, and what the person does in the studio to close it (confirm assumptions and scenarios, review the ledger, pick parts, generate a concept mesh). Results are screening only, V2 at most. Never call the studio ready when the checklist says it is not. The handover is the person's decision. If the gateway lets agents decide a person's steps (the owner set `METAFORGE_ALLOW_AGENT_HUMAN_AUTHORITY`) and your connection names you and the person you act for (`X-MetaForge-Agent`, `X-MetaForge-On-Behalf-Of`), the run is delegated: do not stop. Accept the open assumptions and confirm the inferred scenarios with `twin.imagine_accept`; make the concept mesh with `twin.render_concept_image` then `twin.generate_concept_mesh` (poll `twin.job_status`); hand over with `twin.imagine_handover` once the checklist is ready; and record each of these with `twin.record_decision` as yours on the person's behalf. Otherwise, or if a tool is refused, held or missing, ask the person to accept in the studio and press Hand over to engineering in the dashboard.

When the studio is already handed over and the person asks to go on, follow the skill's After handover section: read the handed-over records, then develop in order (concept mesh, mechanical parts, electronics and firmware against the interface contracts). For each mechanical part, write the CadQuery yourself with the `generate_cad_script` skill (`cadquery.execute_script`, then `twin.commit_geometry`, and `twin.render_work_product` to see it) rather than `twin.design_cad_loop`, which runs its models on the gateway and spends the gateway's model budget. Then Check: measure each validated part by reference with `twin.evaluate_dfm` (it measures what has no exact analysis yet) or `twin.measure_dfm` with its `item_key`, never by passing STEP text; resolve each concept component the release checklist names with `twin.resolve_concept_component` (`realised_by` the CAD items that make it, or `retired`, with a reason); check constraints; and run the structural analyses the requirements call for with `twin.run_item_fea` (mesh then solve on a CAD item, recorded with `twin.record_document`). Run `kicad.run_erc` and `kicad.run_drc` and record each result with `twin.record_evidence` (`producer.tool` the KiCad tool, `depends_on` the schematic or board revision, such as `PCB-LAMP@2`). Then put the product together and check it fits (DFA): commit each board's 3D model (`kicad.export_3d`, then `twin.commit_geometry` with `source_tool: kicad.export_3d` and the board as its source), build the assembly with `twin.build_assembly` (every part and board at its placement, each hinge as a revolute joint with its limits, and in `fasteners` every screw and hinge pin by designation, such as `ISO 4762 M2.5x8` or `ISO 2338 4x30`, placed in the holes it goes through, with its washers and nut: a hole pattern or hinge with no fastener is an unfastened joint unless it is a snap or a press fit), then `twin.check_assembly` with the hole patterns, edge connectors (by `reference`, so the footprint's mating axis comes from the board file, or with `mating_axis`; and the slot with `slot_normal` out of the part and `slot_size_mm`, which a USB-C plug envelope must pass), washer stacks and seated parts the design has, and fix what it finds. Then Make: `twin.get_release_checklist`, the fabrication outputs, each recorded with `twin.record_design_files` and its `source_ref` (the revision it was made from: an output made from an older revision blocks the release), the BOM recorded twice with `twin.record_design_files` (`work_product_type: bom`): the board's from `kicad.export_bom` with `source_ref` the `PCB-...@n`, and the assembly's listing every made part and every fastener by designation and quantity with `source_ref` the `ASM-...@n` (an empty BOM fails the release); every board and schematic recorded as its item's revision, since one that is not is left out and blocks the package; `twin.create_baseline`, a release approval request (`twin.record_engineering_entity`, `entity_type: release_approval`), and `twin.create_release_package` once the checklist, baseline and approval pass. The baseline and the release approval are the person's unless the owner also set `METAFORGE_ALLOW_AGENT_RELEASE` (separate from the human-authority switch) and your connection names you and the person: then create the baseline, record the release approval request, accept a failing ERC, DRC or assembly finding only when it is truly acceptable, with `twin.accept_release_finding` and a reason, approve the release with `twin.approve_release` (refused, with the blockers named, while any other checklist item is open) and create the package, each recorded as yours on the person's behalf. Otherwise stop after the release approval request and ask the person to approve the baseline and the release. In a delegated run carry on through Develop, Check and Make without stopping to ask, recording each decision you take for the person. Writes may be held for approval; an approval that waits for the person stops the run, so say what is waiting and stop.
